The Roadtrip — Days 1 to 5

Five lodging-to-lodging legs through the West Country, deliberately routed off the motorways and onto the spiritual roads of southern England — A272, B3135, A3052, B3157. Each day pairs the must-see, a hidden gem, an outdoors pick, and one classic local plate.

01

Gatwick → Bath via the South Downs

Wed, Jun 3 → Thu, Jun 4 · 1 night · The Ancient Path
🚗 ~280 km · ~4h 30min driving (scenic)
Charlcombe Inn
Lansdown, Bath, BA1 9BT
Conf: see Notes app · Check-in 3 PM · Free breakfast
Lansdown sits on the hill north of Bath — quieter than the center, 10 min by car or 25 min downhill on foot.

The pro move: skip the M25/M4 corridor. Pick up the BMW at Gatwick at 8 AM and head south to the A272 — the spiritual road of southern England, threading the South Downs through villages of yew, flint, and chalk. It's slow on purpose. The scenic route adds a couple of hours but the day's payoff is one big stop: Avebury.

🌾 En route

  • The stop Avebury — go here, not Stonehenge. Stonehenge is a fenced-off circus you view from a path; Avebury is the largest stone circle in Europe and you can walk amongst the stones. The Red Lion pub sits literally inside the circle — pint, ploughman's lunch, allow 2–3 hours total.

🏛️ In Bath

Park at the hotel and walk. Bath is a nightmare for cars — narrow Georgian streets, residents-only zones, and the few central garages run £20+ per day. The Charlcombe Inn has free parking; everything in town is 25 min downhill or a £8 taxi.

  • Iconic Roman Baths — book a timed slot. Audio tour included; allow 90 min. Don't skip the King's Bath terrace.
  • Iconic Royal Crescent & The Circus — the postcard Georgian terraces, free to admire. Pop into No. 1 Royal Crescent (the museum) if you have time.
  • Hidden gem Beckford's Tower — a quirky 1820s neo-classical tower a 10-min walk from your inn, with panoramic views.
  • Hidden gem Prior Park Landscape Garden (NT) — Palladian bridge, gentle valley walk, far less crowded than the Crescent.
  • Outdoors Bath Skyline Walk (NT, 6 mi) — woodland paths above the city; brilliant late-afternoon light.
  • Romance Thermae Bath Spa — modern thermal pool with rooftop overlooking the abbey. Book the evening session.

🍽️ Where to eat

  • Tonight The Scallop Shell — the best fish and chips in the region. No frills, near the river. Exactly what you want after the long drive.
  • Lunch Sally Lunn's Buns — historic eating-house (1680s), home of the original Sally Lunn bun.
  • Splurge alt. The Olive Tree (Michelin-starred, modern British) — book 2 weeks ahead.
  • Casual alt. The Marlborough Tavern — the gastropub locals send visitors to.
Reserve ahead for today: Roman Baths timed entry · Thermae Bath Spa session · Scallop Shell (walk-ins possible but expect a wait at 7 PM) · Olive Tree if swapping it in.
02

Bath → Exeter via Cheddar & Wells

Thu, Jun 4 → Fri, Jun 5 · 1 night · Gorges & Cathedrals
🚗 ~180 km · ~3h 45min driving (scenic)
Hotel Du Vin Exeter
Magdalen Street, Exeter, EX2 4HY
Conf: see Notes app · Non-refundable · Check-in 3 PM
Walking distance to the cathedral and the Quayside. The bistro has a strong wine list — worth a glass even if you eat elsewhere.

Three set-piece stops on the drive. Take the B3135 through Cheddar Gorge — limestone hairpins climbing out of England's deepest natural canyon, walls 137m on either side. Drop into Wells for the cathedral's scissor arches. Then the A39 along the edge of the Quantock Hills for the run into Devon. Skip Glastonbury unless you want crystals and tie-dye — the surrounding countryside is far more rewarding.

🌾 En route

  • Drive Cheddar Gorge (B3135) — the hairpins are spectacular. Pull over at the top viewpoint. The Cheddar Gorge cheese cave is the only place in the world that still ages cheddar in the gorge itself; pop in for a tasting.
  • Stop Wells — the smallest city in England. The cathedral's scissor arches (added in 1338 to stop the central tower collapsing) are a structural marvel like nothing else in Europe. Allow 60–90 min.
  • Scenic A39 along the Quantocks — bracken, wild ponies, sea glimpses on your right. Coleridge wrote Kubla Khan in a farmhouse just off this road.

🏛️ In Exeter

  • Iconic Exeter Cathedral — the longest unbroken medieval vaulted ceiling in the world. Book the rooftop tour.
  • Iconic Underground Passages — guided tour of medieval water-supply tunnels under the city. 45 min, properly atmospheric.
  • Hidden gem Stepcote Hill — the medieval cobbled entrance to the city, used since Saxon times. Walk down it on the way to the Quay; you'll feel the centuries underfoot.
  • Hidden gem Topsham village — 5 mi south, riverside Georgian houses, antique shops, four pubs along a single quay.
  • Outdoors Exe Estuary Trail — flat path along the water from Exeter Quay; bike rentals at Saddles & Paddles.

🍽️ Where to eat

  • Devon classic Cream tea at any tearoom — scones with clotted cream and jam. The Devon way is cream first, then jam (vs. Cornwall's jam-first).
  • Dinner Rusty Bike — gastropub, local meat from their own farm. 10-min walk from the cathedral.
  • Casual The Fish Shed at Topsham — paper-wrapped fish & chips eaten by the river.
  • Splurge Lympstone Manor (Michelin) — 20 min south, Michael Caines' country house.
Reserve ahead for today: Underground Passages tour · Cathedral rooftop tour · Lympstone Manor if doing the splurge.
03

Exeter → Plush via the Jurassic Coast

Fri, Jun 5 → Sat, Jun 6 · 1 night · The Jurassic Backroads
🚗 ~140 km · ~3h 30min driving (coastal)
The Brace of Pheasants
Plush, Dorchester, DT2 7RQ
Conf: see Notes app · Free breakfast · Check-in 4 PM
Plush sits deep in the Piddle Valley — a hamlet of about 60 people. The inn is the village. Phone signal is patchy.

Take the A3052 coastal road — narrow, slow, sea views to the south. Two perfect stops, then a finale on what's arguably the best coastal drive in England.

The Piddle Valley warning. The last 30 minutes into Plush are single-track lanes between hedges taller than the car. Look for the white "passing places" signs — pull into the next one when you see oncoming traffic. Don't reverse down a hill if you can avoid it. Take it slow, you'll be fine.

🌊 En route

  • Stop 1 Beer — a tiny fishing village in a limestone gap (named for the Anglo-Saxon for grove, no relation to the drink). Get a crab sandwich on the beach — the boats land their catch right onto the pebbles.
  • Stop 2 Lyme Regis — fossil hunting on the beach (you'll find ammonites; pick up a hammer and bag for £3 from the harbour shop). Walk the Cobb, the curving harbour wall famous from Persuasion and The French Lieutenant's Woman.
  • Drive B3157, Bridport → Weymouth — arguably the best coastal views in England. Chesil Beach below you, the long pebble bar curving 18 miles to Portland. Pull over at Burton Bradstock or Abbotsbury.

🏛️ Around Plush

  • Iconic Cerne Abbas Giant — the 55m chalk figure on the hillside, 6 km from your bed. Best viewpoint is the marked layby on the A352 just north of the village.
  • Hidden gem Cerne Abbas village — Tudor cottages, abbey ruins, a tiny pond. The Royal Oak is a 14th-c. coaching inn for a pint.
  • Hidden gem Hardy's Cottage (NT) — Thomas Hardy's birthplace, a thatched cob building in the woods. Closes 5 PM.
  • Outdoors Plush ridge walk — a 4-mile circular straight from the inn's door, over Plush Hill with valley views.

🍽️ Where to eat

  • Lunch en route Crab sandwich on Beer beach — you'll smell the boats before you see them.
  • Tonight The Brace of Pheasants itself — book a table even though you're staying upstairs. Roast Dorset lamb, fresh-caught crab, proper game pie.
  • Dorset classic Dorset apple cake with clotted cream — order it for dessert.
Reserve ahead for today: Dinner table at Brace of Pheasants — single most important reservation of the roadtrip. Phone the inn directly: there's no real alternative within a 20-min drive.
04

Plush → Winchester via the New Forest

Sat, Jun 6 → Sun, Jun 7 · 1 night · Hill Forts & Holy Sites
🚗 ~110 km · ~2h 30min driving
Kyoto Rooms
Bridge St, Winchester, SO23 0HN
Conf: see Notes app · Non-refundable · Front desk closes 5:30 PM
⚠️ Front desk only open 2:30–5:30 PM. If you'll arrive later, message the property the day before with your ETA.

Set an alarm. Hike Maiden Castle at dawn — Europe's largest Iron Age hillfort, 15 minutes from Plush. The morning mist on the ramparts is properly haunting and you'll likely have it to yourself. Then drive east through the New Forest via Beaulieu (pronounced "Bewlee"), an ancient royal hunting ground. Watch for wild ponies on the road — they have the right of way and they know it.

🌅 En route

  • Dawn Maiden Castle — the largest Iron Age hill fort in Europe, just south of Dorchester. Free, dramatic, often empty at first light. Allow an hour to walk the inner ramparts.
  • Drive The New Forest via Beaulieu — semi-wild ponies and donkeys grazing alongside the road; speed limit 40 mph for animal welfare. Stop at Beaulieu village (and the National Motor Museum if you're a car person).

🏛️ In Winchester

  • Iconic The Great Hall — see "King Arthur's Round Table" hanging on the wall. (It's a 13th-c. medieval recreation, but still fantastic.) Free entry.
  • Iconic Winchester Cathedral — Jane Austen is buried in the north aisle. Free Evensong at 5:30 PM is the unmissable move.
  • Outdoors Keats' Walk to St Cross — Keats wrote To Autumn walking this exact path along the water meadows. End at the Hospital of St Cross (England's oldest charitable institution) and ask for the "Wayfarer's Dole" at the porter's gate: bread and ale, a tradition since 1136.
  • Hidden gem Wolvesey Castle ruins — free, atmospheric, nearly always empty.
  • Outdoors St Catherine's Hill — short uphill to a turf maze with the best view of Winchester.

🍽️ Where to eat

  • Splurge The Chesil Rectory — the oldest surviving house in Winchester (1450), modern British menu. Book 2 weeks ahead.
  • Classic The Wykeham Arms — wonky old pub, schoolboy desks for tables, near the cathedral. Properly local.
  • Lunch The Black Rat (Michelin) — for a quiet two-person tasting menu.
  • Picnic Sandwich from the Saturday farmers' market, eat in the water meadows at the end of Keats' Walk.
Reserve ahead for today: The Chesil Rectory dinner · Cathedral tower tour. (Round Table, Wolvesey, St Cross, Maiden Castle are all walk-up / free.)

Sun, Jun 7 · transfer day

The day with most logistics. SIXT closes the deal on the BMW at Gatwick by 17:00. From Gatwick, take the Gatwick Express (30 min, every 15 min) to Victoria, then the District line to Hammersmith (~25 min). Total transit: about 75 minutes from car return to checking into the Premier Inn.

  • 11:00 AM — drive Winchester → Gatwick (1h 45min via M3/M25).
  • 1:00 PM — return car at Gatwick (well before the 5 PM deadline; gives a buffer).
  • 2:00 PM — Gatwick Express to Victoria (£23 standard, cheaper online).
  • 3:30 PM — check into Premier Inn Hammersmith.
  • Evening — see the London section below.
Days 5 — 7 · Three nights

London

Premier Inn Hammersmith · 181 Talgarth Road · W6 8DN · Conf: see Notes app

Hammersmith is a quiet, leafy base with riverside pubs and excellent tube connections — Piccadilly line goes direct to Covent Garden in 20 min, District line to Westminster in 25 min. The Thames Path runs right past the hotel.

Day 5 · Sun, Jun 7 Arrival evening

Low-key after the drive and the trains. Walk 12 minutes south from the hotel to Hammersmith Bridge and follow the Thames Path west to The Dove — a 17th-century riverside pub where Charles II is said to have entertained Nell Gwynn. Have dinner at the river. If you want a bit more energy, hop the District line one stop to Ravenscourt Park and the gastropub The Anglesea Arms.

Tonight: book a Thames-side table at The Dove or River Café for sunset.

Day 6 · Mon, Jun 8 The Greatest Hits

  • Morning British Museum (free) — opens 10 AM, focus on the Rosetta Stone, the Parthenon Marbles, and the Egyptian galleries. 2.5 hrs is plenty.
  • Lunch Dishoom Covent Garden — book the bacon naan for breakfast or the lamb raan for lunch.
  • Afternoon Westminster walk — Westminster Abbey (book or do free 5 PM Evensong), Big Ben, Parliament Square, walk across the Thames to the South Bank.
  • Outdoors South Bank stroll — Tate Modern (free), the Globe, Borough Market (closes 5 PM), Tower Bridge.
  • Dinner St. John Smithfield — Fergus Henderson's nose-to-tail temple, the menu the world copied. Or Padella for a 35-min queue and £10 hand-rolled pasta.

Day 7 · Tue, Jun 9 Off the Beaten Path

  • Morning Hampstead Heath + Kenwood House — wild parkland, the swimming ponds, and Kenwood (free, with Vermeer and Rembrandt). End in Highgate Village for coffee.
  • Lunch Maltby Street Market (Sat/Sun only — if you're here Tue, swap for Borough Market or Brick Lane Sunday). For a Tuesday: head to Coal Drops Yard in King's Cross, eat at Caravan.
  • Afternoon Sir John Soane's Museum (free) — the architect's house preserved exactly as he left it, stuffed with sarcophagi. Tiny, magical.
  • Late afternoon Stroll Notting Hill — Portobello Road antiques, pastel houses, mews streets.
  • Dinner The Ledbury (Notting Hill, 3-star Michelin if splurging) or Trinity in Clapham.
Reserve ahead: Tower of London timed entry · West End theater (book the same week if you want a specific show) · St. John Smithfield · The Ledbury (book a month ahead).
Days 8 — 11 · Four nights

Paris

Hotel Des Arts Paris Montmartre · 5 Rue Tholoze · 75018 · Conf: see Notes app

Montmartre is the romantic Paris of cliché, and it earns it — narrow stairs, Rue Tholoze climbs to the Moulin de la Galette, Sacré-Cœur is a 6-min walk uphill. Mornings are quiet; afternoons get crowded with tour groups around the basilica. Plan early starts and slow evenings.

Day 8 · Wed, Jun 10 Arrival in Montmartre

Eurostar London → Paris from St Pancras International (~2h20). Aim for a 9–10 AM departure — you'll be checked into the hotel by mid-afternoon. From Gare du Nord, take Métro line 2 to Anvers, then walk up (or skip the stairs by taking the funicular).

  • Late afternoon Sacré-Cœur — climb to the steps for sunset over the city. Free, but the dome (€8) gives 360° views.
  • Evening Place du Tertre by night (after the painters pack up), then a glass at Le Consulat on the corner.
  • Dinner Le Coq Rico — Antoine Westermann's poultry-only restaurant, two blocks from your hotel. Or the cosier Le Bouillon Pigalle — classic French at €15-a-plate prices.

Day 9 · Thu, Jun 11 Right Bank Classics

  • Morning Louvre at 9 AM — book the timed entry. Go straight to the Mona Lisa first, then circle back to the Venus de Milo, Winged Victory, and the Egyptian wing. 3 hours max.
  • Lunch & walk Walk through the Tuileries to Place de la Concorde, then up the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe (climb it for less crowded views than the tower).
  • Afternoon Sainte-Chapelle — the medieval stained-glass chapel on Île de la Cité, smaller and more spectacular than Notre-Dame's exterior (which you can admire from outside while reconstruction continues).
  • Aperitif A glass at Le Comptoir du Relais (St-Germain) before dinner. The bistro's tasting dinners are booked months out, but the lunch and bar menu are walk-in.
  • Dinner Le Comptoir (if you scored a reservation), Bouillon Chartier for the budget classic, or Septime (11th, two stars, book 3 weeks out at exactly noon Paris time).

Day 10 · Fri, Jun 12 Marais & the East

  • Morning Wander the Marais — Place des Vosges (the oldest planned square in Paris), the Picasso Museum, narrow medieval streets.
  • Lunch L'As du Fallafel on Rue des Rosiers — eat it standing up.
  • Afternoon Père Lachaise Cemetery — Oscar Wilde, Jim Morrison, Chopin, Édith Piaf. Pick up a map at the entrance.
  • Late afternoon Walk down Canal Saint-Martin with a glass of wine from a bar à vin, sit on the iron footbridges. Or up to Belleville for the best view of the city without the Montmartre crowds.
  • Dinner Frenchie (book months ahead) or its walk-in sibling Frenchie Bar à Vins across the alley.

Day 11 · Sat, Jun 13 Versailles or Left Bank

Choose one — both are great, neither fits in the same day comfortably.

  • Option A · Versailles RER C from Champ de Mars, ~40 min. Book the Passport with timed entry. Be there at opening (9 AM); do the State Apartments first, then the gardens. The fountain show runs Saturdays in June (€11 extra). Back in Paris by 5 PM.
  • Option B · Slow Left Bank Musée d'Orsay (Impressionists; smaller and friendlier than the Louvre) → walk through St-Germain → Luxembourg Gardens picnic → Shakespeare & Company bookshop on the Seine → Eiffel Tower at sunset from Trocadéro (the picture spot) or up the tower itself.
  • Last dinner La Maison Rose in Montmartre (the pink corner restaurant Utrillo painted) for atmosphere, or Clamato (11th) for seafood-leaning small plates.
Reserve ahead: Eurostar (urgently) · Louvre 9 AM slot · Eiffel summit (sunset) · Versailles Passport · Septime, Frenchie, Le Comptoir du Relais (each at least 3 weeks out).

Day 12 · Sun, Jun 14 Departure

Check-out by 11 AM. Save a final pastry stop for Du Pain et des Idées (10th) or Pierre Hermé (4th) on the way out. Au revoir, Paris.
